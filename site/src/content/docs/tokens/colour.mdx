## Semantic roles — light

The vocabulary components speak. A role is always paired with the on-colour legible on
top of it, which is what makes the contract platform-neutral: any platform with a notion
of "a colour and the colour that goes on it" can host the binding.

<GapNotice
  title="The brand roles are placeholders"
  prefix="color"
  match={(t) => t.description?.includes('PLACEHOLDER')}
  label="placeholders"
>
They alias the **neutral ramp**: there is no chromatic brand accent yet, so they are named
slots awaiting a palette. That is why several of them resolve to the same value as roles
they are supposed to differ from.

This is the single most consequential open decision in the spec. Filling it re-points
those roles and changes the appearance of every implementation built against the
placeholders. Tracked as [`flowin_pm#30`](https://github.com/luisburgos/flowin_pm/issues/30).
</GapNotice>

### Why the repeated values matter

The **Resolves to** column flags roles that share a value. Those are not duplicates to be
tidied up — they are roles the spec deliberately distinguishes that happen to land on the
same neutral today.

That distinction is invisible to any rendering test: a binding pointed at the wrong role
renders correctly and stays wrong until the accent lands. The 2026-08 audit found exactly
this twice, and only by reading role *names* rather than comparing pixels.

## Status roles

`success`, `warning` and `info`, each with an on-colour. Unlike the core roles these have
**no universal native slot** — a platform's colour table typically models a primary, a
surface and an error and stops there — so they bind through the platform's
theme-extension mechanism instead.

`info` is neutral rather than chromatic: it is not an alert, and under the monochrome
thesis colour is reserved for meaning.

## Primitive ramps

The raw palette. Never consumed directly by a component — semantic roles alias these.

<Aside title="Monochrome by design">
The `neutral` ramp carries no hue, and there are no brand ramps here at all. Colour in
this system is reserved for *meaning*: only `error`, `warning` and `success` are
chromatic. When nothing else on screen is coloured, a red or a green is impossible to
miss.

Three same-valued brand ramps read like placeholder scaffolding, so this is recorded
explicitly: the greys are a decision, not an unfinished palette.
</Aside>
